# Evaluation goal

Evaluate the significance of literary news for readers, writers, and publishing professionals.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Notable.** Major prizes, landmark publications or posthumous releases, archival discoveries of real literary-historical importance, or industry changes with broad consequences for authors and readers.
- **7-8: Significant.** Strong critical events, awards shortlists of major prizes, significant imprint or translation program changes, and consequential debates with substance.
- **5-6: Worth reading.** New publications of solid interest, mid-tier prize news, worthwhile essays or interviews, and routine but real industry developments.
- **3-4: Minor.** Listicles, celebrity-book coverage, routine paperback releases, or promotional interviews without substance.
- **0-2: Noise.** Gossip without literary relevance, pure marketing, or off-topic material.

# Evaluation guidance

Literary value is partly judgment, so weight durability and consequence: does the item change what readers can read, how work is valued, or understanding of a writer? Prefer named critics and specific arguments over adjectives. Coverage volume alone is not significance.
