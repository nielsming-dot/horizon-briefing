# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether a medical news item gives readers timely, credible, and clinically meaningful information about disease, treatment, public health, or health policy.


# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Practice-changing.** Results or approvals that alter standard of care, landmark trials with clear outcome benefits, guideline changes, or public-health events with broad consequences.
- **7-8: Important.** Notable trial readouts, new approvals in underserved conditions, credible safety signals, or policy changes affecting large patient groups.
- **5-6: Useful.** Early-phase findings, mechanistic insights with plausible clinical paths, observational studies with clear limitations, or approvals with modest incremental value.
- **3-4: Low value.** Preclinical or animal findings presented without context, small uncontrolled studies, press-release science without data, or routine administrative updates.
- **0-2: Noise.** Health misinformation, unverifiable claims, promotional content, or material with no medical substance.

# Evaluation guidance

Weight evidence quality heavily: randomized trials and meta-analyses over observational data, peer-reviewed publications over press releases, absolute effects over relative risks without baselines. Check whether endpoints are clinical outcomes or surrogate markers, and whether the sponsor or conflicts are disclosed. Do not reward hype around early-phase results, and treat "in mice" findings as low priority unless the method itself is the news.
