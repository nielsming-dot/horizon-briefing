# Evaluation goal

Evaluate the importance of physics developments for researchers and technically informed readers.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Field-defining.** Results that confirm, overturn, or sharply constrain major theories, first-of-kind experimental regimes, or facilities opening new measurement capability.
- **7-8: Significant.** Notable experimental advances, precision measurements improving on prior limits, well-supported theoretical results with testable predictions, or reproducible records in materials or quantum systems.
- **5-6: Interesting.** Incremental measurements, proof-of-concept demos, methodological improvements, or results of interest mainly to a subfield.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Routine instrumentation updates, results within expected theory without new discriminating power, or popularized coverage with little quantitative content.
- **0-2: Noise.** Speculation without calculation or measurement, promotional claims, sensational misreadings of preprints, or off-topic material.

# Evaluation guidance

Consider whether the result is quantitatively characterized (uncertainties, baselines, comparison with theory or prior measurements), whether it is peer-reviewed or replicated, and how much of the field's parameter or theory space it constrains. Reward first observations and definitive constraints over marginal improvements, and treat claims of "new physics" without statistical significance as low value.
