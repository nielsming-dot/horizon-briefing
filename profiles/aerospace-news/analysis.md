# Evaluation goal

Evaluate the importance of spaceflight and aerospace developments for readers who follow missions, the launch industry, and space policy.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Historic.** First launches or landings of major vehicles, human spaceflight milestones, mission failures or successes with program-level consequences, or decisions that reshape the industry.
- **7-8: Important.** Notable mission outcomes, significant contract awards, vehicle test milestones with meaningful data, major schedule or program changes, and consequential regulatory decisions.
- **5-6: Interesting.** Routine successful launches with secondary objectives, incremental vehicle updates, personnel announcements, or mission progress reports.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Manifest shuffles without consequence, promotional renders and concept art, routine booster recoveries now commonplace, or speculation about far-future plans.
- **0-2: Noise.** Rumors without sourcing, conspiracy material, or off-topic content.

# Evaluation guidance

Consider program-level consequence over spectacle: a first orbital attempt or a crewed mission anomaly matters more than a routine cargo run. Weigh official confirmations over social-media claims, note whether milestones are achieved or merely announced, and treat recurring launch cadence as background unless something distinguishes the flight.
