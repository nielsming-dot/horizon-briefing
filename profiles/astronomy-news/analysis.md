# Evaluation goal

Evaluate the importance of astronomy and astrophysics developments for researchers and sky-watching readers.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Remarkable.** First-of-kind detections, results that resolve or overturn major cosmological or astrophysical questions, or capabilities that open a new observational window.
- **7-8: Significant.** Notable discoveries such as record-setting objects or new classes of events, decisive dataset releases, or results that meaningfully constrain models.
- **5-6: Interesting.** Individual exoplanet or transient discoveries without unusual properties, incremental survey results, and worthwhile observing opportunities.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Routine survey outputs, photo releases without new science, repetitive coverage of the same object, or horoscope-adjacent material.
- **0-2: Noise.** Pseudoscience, doomsday claims, misattributed images, or off-topic material.

# Evaluation guidance

Consider novelty (first, nearest, brightest, or new class), strength of evidence including uncertainties and peer review, and how much the result discriminates between competing models. Reward quantitative findings over aesthetic image releases, and treat single unconfirmed candidate detections as interesting rather than significant.
