# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether chemical products market news gives readers timely, quantified, and decision-relevant information about prices, margins, capacity, trade, or demand for commodity chemicals and polymers.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Market-moving.** Major capacity startups or permanent closures at scale, trade measures (anti-dumping duties, quotas) with product and volume specifics, price dislocations with clearly attributed causes, or landmark investment decisions.
- **7-8: Important.** Notable price or margin shifts with drivers and baselines, significant turnaround or force-majeure events affecting supply, capacity milestones with volumes, credible supply-demand rebalancing analysis, and consequential feedstock cost changes.
- **5-6: Useful.** Routine price monitoring with context, smaller capacity changes, monthly trade data, and moderate demand indicators with figures.
- **3-4: Low value.** Price mentions without direction or cause, promotional or brokerage commentary, vague forecasts without methodology, and repetitive coverage of the same move.
- **0-2: Noise.** Unverified rumors, marketing content, or material with no chemical products substance.

# Evaluation guidance

Reward numbers: prices or price changes with units and comparison basis (%, USD/t, CNY/t), capacities (kt/yr, Mt/yr), utilization rates, import/export volumes. Distinguish confirmed events (startup, shutdown, duty imposed) from expectations and rumors. For China-related items, note the policy or trade context when it drives the market. A bare price list is not news; a price move with its driver and downstream effect is.
