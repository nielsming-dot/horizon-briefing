Early community testing reports plausible coding behavior with decode speeds of roughly 27 tokens/s on a Ryzen 395 via llama.cpp Vulkan and about 10 tokens/s on a 32GB M1 Pro MacBook with q4 quantization. hackernews · jasondavies · Nov 20, 20:12 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50581260)

**「Background」** Looped transformer architectures reuse the same block of layers multiple times per forward pass, trading additional sequential computation for a smaller parameter count — an approach some frontier models are only rumored to use, per community discussion. Thinking Machines&\#x27; release makes this design openly available with end-to-end nvfp4 \(4-bit floating point\) training, rather than post-training quantization, and the model&\#x27;s sizing was stated by the team to target the local-inference community&\#x27;s most requested configurations. Reported community benchmarks place it near Qwen3.8-27B class performance, though these are self-reported and community-run tests, not independent evaluations.

**「Practical impact」** For local inference builders, the nvfp4-native weights and looped architecture currently require workarounds: llama.cpp support is incomplete, with several relevant PRs \(32694, 32657, 32429, 32704\) unmerged and a small source patch reported to yield about 30% faster decoding. Performance claims should be treated as provisional until broader independent evaluation confirms whether the Qwen3.8-27B-level benchmarks hold in real-world workloads.

### [2 million canary prompts expose widespread leaks in AI gateway supply chain](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/a-leaky-ai-supply-chain-is-exposing-secret-user-data-at-an-alarming-rate/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Security researcher HD Moore planted 2 million canary secrets—never-before-used AWS access keys, SSH passwords, unique URLs, DNS names, and private git repository references—into prompts routed through AI gateway services such as OpenRouter and Vercel, which broker end-user prompts to inference engines. Because each secret appeared in exactly one prompt to a single provider and Moore controlled the infrastructure receiving any callbacks, any triggered canary identified the specific link in the AI service chain that leaked it. According to Ars Technica, the experiment found the ecosystem leaks highly sensitive user data at an alarming rate, though the excerpt does not quantify the leak rate or name the specific providers responsible.

rss · Ars Technica · Nov 20, 20:59

**「What AI routers are and why they matter」** Many AI applications today do not call model providers like OpenAI or Anthropic directly; instead, developers use intermediaries such as OpenRouter or Vercel, known as AI routers or AI gateways, which receive end-user prompts and forward them to an inference engine chosen by criteria like lowest price or task fit. This adds multiple third parties to every request, meaning sensitive user data can pass through providers the end user never chose and may not know about. Researcher HD Moore ran the canary experiment over roughly five months, from July through November 2026, seeding each of about two million unique, previously unused secrets into a single prompt sent to one provider so any leak could be traced to a specific link in the chain.

**「Why it matters」** Developers who route prompts through AI gateways cannot assume user-submitted secrets—API keys, credentials, and private repository references—remain with the intended provider; the canary method gives them a way to test their own prompt traffic for leaks before entrusting real credentials to these intermediaries.

### [Researchers Prove Exact Criterion for Perfectly Resilient Network Routing](https://www.quantamagazine.org/network-researchers-nail-down-theory-of-perfect-resilience-20261120/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Matthias Bentert and Stefan Schmid have settled a long-standing question in theoretical computer science by proving exactly when networks can achieve &quot;perfect resilience&quot; — the ability to deliver packets between any two points under any pattern of link failures short of full disconnection. In a paper presented at the Symposium on Theory of Computing in June 2026, they showed there are exactly four minimal graph structures \(forbidden minors\) that obstruct perfect resilience.
