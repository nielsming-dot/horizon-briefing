Researchers discovered credit card skimming scripts on popular shopping sites that exfiltrate stolen payment data over WebRTC, a channel that is monitored less closely than typical HTTP traffic. The campaign was reported on November 27, 2026, around Black Friday, when e-commerce traffic and fraud risk peak. The report notes the attackers specifically target mobile devices, which are harder to inspect. Details such as the exact number of affected sites and named victims were not provided in the available source material.

hackernews · scanr · Nov 27, 15:40 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50668145)

**「Background」** WebRTC \(Web Real-Time Communication\) is a browser API built for peer-to-peer media like video calls, and its data channels often bypass the HTTP monitoring tools that security teams use to inspect outbound traffic. That makes it attractive as an exfiltration path for e-commerce skimming, the Magecart-style class of attacks in which compromised or third-party JavaScript injected into checkout pages silently reads payment card fields. Previous skimmer campaigns have typically used ordinary HTTPS POST requests to send stolen card data to attacker servers, so defenses have focused on inspecting and restricting those requests rather than WebRTC traffic.

**「What this means for shoppers and merchants」** Shoppers on affected sites—especially mobile users, who attackers reportedly target because they are harder to detect—should treat card details entered through embedded checkout scripts as potentially exposed and monitor statements for unauthorized charges, particularly during the Black Friday shopping peak. For merchants, the incident underscores that embedding third-party JavaScript creates a persistent exfiltration risk, so security teams should audit and restrict third-party scripts and extend monitoring to less-inspected channels like WebRTC, not just conventional HTTP traffic.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters debated the root cause: afavour argued that WebRTC itself is not the problem, since the same exfiltration could be done over plain HTTP — the real exposure is embedding any third-party JavaScript, which means sites running such scripts are always vulnerable. Others went further: snickerer called running stranger-supplied code in the browser a design flaw of the web itself, s\_u\_d\_o asked why ad providers do not sandbox ads without client-side JS execution, and jszymborski said the incident justifies using ad blockers. \_\_MatrixMan\_\_ speculated that payment systems still rely on symmetric secrets rather than asymmetric cryptography because payment intermediaries benefit from controlling transaction reversals — an opinion, not an established fact.

### [KM3NeT underwater detector constrains Lorentz invariance violation with 18 months of data](https://physicsworld.com/a/underwater-neutrino-detector-sets-new-limits-on-einsteins-special-relativity/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The KM3NeT collaboration has used 18 months of data from its partially completed ORCA undersea neutrino telescope, located about 40 km off Toulon, France at roughly 2450 m depth, to set new limits on possible violations of Lorentz invariance, the principle underlying special relativity. By comparing the energies and arrival directions of atmospheric neutrinos with standard oscillation predictions, the team found no evidence of violations but placed upper limits on a broad range of Lorentz-violating parameters, published in the Journal of Cosmology and Astroparticle Physics. The constraints are comparable to results from Super-Kamiokande&\#x27;s 12-year dataset and IceCube&\#x27;s two-year analysis, and include the first direct constraints on parameters describing possible differences between muon and tau neutrino flavours. The collaboration notes the effects, if they exist, may simply be too small for the current dataset to detect.

rss · Physics World · Nov 27, 10:00

**「Background: neutrinos as tests of special relativity」** Lorentz invariance, incorporated into Einstein&\#x27;s 1905 special relativity, states that the laws of physics are identical for all inertial observers. Reconciling gravity with quantum field theory remains an open problem, and some quantum-gravity approaches suggest that tiny Lorentz-violating deviations could appear as observable signatures in neutrino propagation. Because neutrinos travel vast distances, span enormous energy ranges, and oscillate between flavours, even minute deviations could alter their energies and arrival directions — which is why KM3NeT&\#x27;s underwater telescopes, ORCA off Toulon and ARCA off Sicily, can search for them using atmospheric and astrophysical neutrinos.

**「Competitive sensitivity from a partially built detector」** The result demonstrates that ORCA can compete with established experiments using only its first six detector units, out of a planned 108, narrowing the range of theoretical models compatible with observation and pointing to stronger constraints as detector statistics grow. Because Lorentz-violating effects on neutrino propagation grow with distance travelled, ARCA&\#x27;s astrophysical neutrinos should provide still more sensitive probes once the full detector array is complete.

rss · Phys.org Space · Nov 27, 17:00

**「Why this discrepancy matters」** Dark matter does not emit light, so astronomers infer its distribution indirectly from the motions of stars and gas within galaxies, whose dynamics trace the total gravitational field. Cosmological simulations of galaxy formation make concrete predictions for how much of this unseen matter should reside in the inner regions of galaxies of a given mass. The new analysis of 136 nearby galaxies compares such dynamically inferred dark matter content against those simulation predictions, and the reported shortfall in the inner regions, which grows with galaxy mass, directly probes how well current models reproduce the structure of real galaxies.

**「Impact」** The mass-dependent nature of the discrepancy provides a concrete test for galaxy-formation simulations, which will need to reproduce both the deficit of inner dark matter and its scaling with galaxy mass to remain consistent with observed galaxy dynamics.

rss · Phys.org Space · Nov 27, 13:40

**「Background」** The standard cosmological model \(Lambda-CDM\) predicts that dwarf galaxies form within extended halos of cold dark matter, and the size and concentration of a dwarf&\#x27;s inner dark matter distribution is a long-standing diagnostic used to test those predictions. UGCA 320 is a nearby, gas-rich dwarf galaxy observed as part of the MHONGOOSE survey, whose neutral hydrogen \(HI\) radio data from MeerKAT traces the galaxy&\#x27;s rotation out to large radii, while MUSE H-alpha spectroscopy constrains the inner kinematics at high resolution. Combining both tracers, the preprint&\#x27;s authors report an exceptionally extended dark matter core reaching far beyond the galaxy&\#x27;s stars, a configuration they argue is difficult to reconcile with standard model expectations \(tool-2-2, tool-2-3\).

**「Impact」** If the large core is confirmed, it would place new observational constraints on how dark matter behaves in dwarf galaxies, motivating follow-up observations of UGCA 320 and reassessment of dark matter models that predict smaller cores in such low-mass systems.

rss · Space.com · Nov 27, 11:00

**「What luminous red novae are」** AT2025abao is a luminous red nova, a transient produced when two stars in a binary system merge, ejecting gas and dust that cools and radiates strongly in infrared light. **「A template for merger precursors」** The multi-year pre-eruption monitoring—showing slow brightening years before outburst and a cooling, dust-enshrouded state months prior—gives transient surveys concrete precursor signatures to search for when identifying stellar mergers before they erupt, and demonstrates the value of coordinated multi-facility follow-up programs.
