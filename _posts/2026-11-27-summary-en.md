A Lancet correspondence by Kambiz Rahbar and Martin Boegemann discusses the PSMAddition phase 3 trial, in which Scott T Tagawa and colleagues reported that adding the radioligand \[177Lu\]Lu-PSMA-617 to androgen-deprivation therapy plus an androgen receptor pathway inhibitor reduced radiographic progression or death in metastatic, androgen pathway modulator-naive/sensitive prostate cancer \(hazard ratio 0.72; 95% CI 0.58-0.90\). The correspondence affirms that this establishes PSMA-targeted radioligand therapy as beneficial in hormone-sensitive disease, but notes the trial did not determine how much treatment each patient needs. Because the item is commentary on the trial rather than the primary report, the findings should be interpreted with reference to the original publication, and the key endpoint was radiographic progression rather than overall survival.

rss · The Lancet · Nov 28, 00:00

**「Background」** Metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer is typically treated first with androgen-deprivation therapy combined with an androgen receptor pathway inhibitor, and PSMA-targeted radioligand therapy with \[177Lu\]Lu-PSMA-617 had previously been established mainly in later, castration-resistant disease. The PSMAddition phase 3 trial moved this radioligand approach earlier, showing that adding it to ADT plus an ARPI reduced radiographic progression or death \(hazard ratio 0.72; 95% CI 0.58–0.90\). The correspondence notes that while the trial supports radioligand therapy in hormone-sensitive disease, it does not resolve how much treatment each individual patient needs.

**「Clinical impact」** If the PSMAddition findings are implemented, radioligand therapy with \[177Lu\]Lu-PSMA-617 may move earlier into the treatment course for patients with hormone-sensitive metastatic prostate cancer who are starting androgen-deprivation therapy plus an androgen receptor pathway inhibitor. However, as the correspondence emphasizes, clinicians currently lack evidence on individualized treatment duration, so questions about how many cycles each patient needs remain unresolved.
