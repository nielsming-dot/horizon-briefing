### [Coldframe: A Python Honeypot Mimicking Supermarket Fridge Controllers](https://github.com/hedgerow-dev/coldframe) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A developer released Coldframe, an open-source, single-file Python honeypot that impersonates supermarket refrigeration controllers, specifically the Danfoss AK-SM 800A and Dixell XWEB web interfaces. Built with only the Python standard library and drawing on public research by Synacktiv, Claroty, and Ironhackers, it replicates real devices&\#x27; fingerprints \(nginx header order, page padding to exactly 8,202 bytes, TLS versions\) closely enough to be recognized on Shodan as genuine hardware rather than a honeypot. It also detects and logs exploit attempts against CVE-2025-41450 — the Danfoss &quot;code of the day&quot; login bypass — plus three other Danfoss CVEs and XWEB vulnerabilities, and serves a fake store dashboard after default logins to observe attackers&\#x27; second moves. The project was inspired by recent incidents involving industrial fridge installations; after one day online, the author&\#x27;s two live instances had seen only generic scanners and Shodan&\#x27;s own crawler.

hackernews · hedgerow-dev · Nov 7, 17:27 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50406292)

**「Background」** Coldframe impersonates two real supermarket refrigeration controllers: the Danfoss AK-SM 800A and the Dixell XWEB. Danfoss disclosed an improper authentication vulnerability in the AK-SM8xxA series in March 2025, fixed by patch 4.2, and Claroty&\#x27;s Team82 published research on multiple AK-SM 800A web interface vulnerabilities in August 2026 — public work that the honeypot draws on to recognize real exploit attempts, including the CVE-2025-41450 &quot;code of the day&quot; login bypass.

**「Why It Matters」** The honeypot gives defenders and researchers a low-cost way to observe real exploitation attempts against widely deployed refrigeration controllers without exposing actual equipment, and its CVE tagging means collected logs directly indicate which known flaws attackers are using. The author argues the deeper problem is structural: supermarkets typically have no security team, so fixes like VPNs and strong passwords must ship by default from vendors or installers rather than depend on dedicated maintenance work.

**「Community Reaction」** Commenter supriyo-biswas cautioned against relying on Shodan data for fingerprinting, reporting that most WiFi cameras they examined online were themselves honeypots or already-compromised devices, and suggested probing a real vulnerable device instead. Meanwhile, commenter xrd questioned whether recent industrial-fridge incidents were definitively hacks at all, noting earlier speculation that a synchronized software update could have resembled an attack — a question the source material does not settle.

### [Rubin Observatory&\#x27;s Early Data Reveals Two Hidden Milky Way Companions](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261104012252.htm) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory has identified two previously unknown companions of the Milky Way from early observations covering only about 7% of the night sky. One is an extremely faint galaxy located roughly 355,000 light-years away, and the other is a star cluster obscured by cosmic dust. These are announced as discoveries from preliminary survey data rather than fully characterized, peer-reviewed results, and detailed properties of the objects have not yet been reported. Researchers suspect that hundreds of additional faint satellite galaxies remain undiscovered and could be revealed as Rubin&\#x27;s survey continues.

rss · ScienceDaily Astronomy · Nov 7, 09:12

**「Why faint satellites matter」** Ultra-faint dwarf galaxies are the smallest and most dark-matter-dominated galaxies known, and they are notoriously hard to find: their sparse stellar populations blend into the sky, and objects on the far side of the Milky Way are additionally obscured by cosmic dust, which is why a dust-hidden star cluster could evade earlier surveys. Cosmological models of dark matter predict that the Milky Way should host many more such faint companions than have been detected so far, so each new satellite discovery constrains those predictions. Rubin&\#x27;s wide-field, high-sensitivity survey is designed to detect exactly these low-surface-brightness objects, which explains why even a preliminary pass covering only 7% of the sky yielded two new companions.

**「Constraining Satellite Population Models」** Finding two new satellites in only a small fraction of Rubin&\#x27;s survey provides early constraints on models of the Milky Way&\#x27;s satellite population and dark matter, and future survey releases should substantially enlarge the known census of faint satellites.

### [NASA Expands Moon Base Program With CLPS 2 Awards, Nuclear Rover, and Transport Contracts](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-awards-moon-base-transportation-technology-science-projects/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

NASA announced a suite of awards and mission updates on Saturday, November 7, 2026, at the MAX POWER aerospace expo at Kennedy Space Center to expand its Moon Base exploration, science, and infrastructure campaign at the Moon&\#x27;s South Pole. The agency selected eight companies—Blue Origin, Firefly Aerospace, Intuitive Machines, iSpace US, Lockheed Martin Space, Northrop Grumman Space Systems, SpaceX, and Voyager Technologies—for the next phase of its Commercial Lunar Payload Services \(CLPS\) initiative under a new CLPS 2 framework, a 10-year, multiple-award indefinite-delivery/indefinite-quantity master contract with a combined maximum value of $10 billion. NASA also confirmed it is moving forward with PROMISE \(Polar Rover for Observation, Mapping, and In-Situ Exploration\), a nuclear-powered, six-wheeled rover that JPL will build largely from spare Curiosity and Perseverance Mars rover hardware, targeting launch in late 2028 for an 18-month mission to characterize surface and subsurface resources, including ice in permanently shadowed regions. Blue Origin received a $94 million base contract, with an additional $140 million performance-based option, to deliver PROMISE to the lunar South Pole on its Mark 1 lander, and NASA awarded six VADR task orders to Arrow Science and Technology, Blue Origin, and Impulse Space for launch and orbital transfer of Moon Base cargo.

rss · NASA News Releases · Nov 7, 22:47

**「Program background」** The awards build on NASA&\#x27;s existing Commercial Lunar Payload Services \(CLPS\) initiative, under which companies build and operate landers that deliver NASA science, technology, and infrastructure payloads to the lunar surface. NASA extended the current CLPS 1.0 contract, raising its value from $2.6 billion to $4.2 billion to support initial Moon Base Phase One objectives, with the original contract running through November 2028 and deliveries completing by November 2033. The new PROMISE rover draws directly on heritage from NASA&\#x27;s Curiosity and Perseverance Mars rovers, which the Jet Propulsion Laboratory will reuse in the form of spare flight components and engineering test models to reduce cost and shorten development time.

**「What It Means」** The CLPS 2 award materially enlarges NASA&\#x27;s lunar delivery pipeline: alongside raising the current CLPS 1.0 contract from $2.6 billion to $4.2 billion \(running through November 2028, with deliveries complete by November 2033\), the new $10 billion framework gives eight vendors eligibility to compete for delivery missions supporting Moon Base Phase One objectives. Reusing proven Mars rover flight spares and operational approaches is intended to reduce cost and shorten PROMISE&\#x27;s integration and testing timeline, while the rover&\#x27;s nuclear power allows operations during long lunar nights and in shadowed regions that conventional solar-powered rovers cannot reliably explore.

### [Esbjerg Seawater Heat Pumps Deliver 55.2 MW of District Heat from 3.3°C North Sea Water](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/07/esbjerg-seawater-heat-pumps-cold-water-district-heating/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Esbjerg, Denmark is operating two large seawater-source heat pumps that supply its district-heating network with heat extracted from the North Sea. During a two-hour winter test, seawater entered the system at 3.3°C and exited at 1.2°C, while the heat pumps consumed 19.9 MW of electricity and delivered 55.2 MW of heat—an implied coefficient of performance of roughly 2.8 at near-freezing intake temperatures. The system is commissioned and measured under test conditions; longer-term performance data were not reported in the source.

rss · CleanTechnica · Nov 7, 17:21

**「Why seawater-source heat pumps matter for district heating」** District heating networks supply heat to buildings through insulated water pipes, and decarbonizing them typically means replacing fossil-fuel boilers with large electric heat pumps that raise low-grade ambient heat to network supply temperatures. Seawater is an attractive heat source because oceans moderate temperature swings and remain relatively warm in winter, even though extracting usable heat from water near 0–4°C challenges heat-pump performance compared with milder sources. Esbjerg&\#x27;s project demonstrates this approach at a scale relevant to Danish municipal heating systems, which are common across Denmark and are a central target for electrification.

**「Relevance for Low-Temperature District Heating」** The measured COP of about 2.8 at 3.3°C seawater demonstrates that near-freezing water bodies can serve as a viable heat source for large district-heating systems, giving Danish utilities and similar coastal networks an operational data point for electrifying heat supply in winter conditions.
