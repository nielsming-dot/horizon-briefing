### [Ant Lingbo open-sources LingBot-Touch, a cross-sensor tactile foundation model](https://www.qbitai.com/2026/11/508996.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

On November 10, 2026, Ant Lingbo \(蚂蚁灵波\) open-sourced LingBot-Touch, a tactile foundation model that encodes signals from three mainstream sensing modalities—magnetic, visuotactile, and piezoresistive—into a unified set of tactile tokens that any downstream action policy can consume. The model was pretrained on roughly 4,983 hours \(about 1.33 billion frames\) of real tactile data, about two-thirds from humans wearing tactile gloves and one-third from teleoperated Sharpa Wave dual-arm robots, using a JEPA-style objective with 90% contact-point masking and RoPE-based spatial addressing to generalize across sensor layouts. In 26 real-robot tasks across Sharpa Wave and XHand1 hands, four action policies \(LingBot-VLA, π0.5, Diffusion Policy, and T-Rex\) showed average success-rate gains of roughly 14 to 26 percentage points with LingBot-Touch versus no tactile input; notably, when fed raw sensor readings instead, π0.5&\#x27;s zipper task success dropped from 39% to 15%. The lightweight LingBot-Touch-Tiny variant \(~5.3 million parameters\) adds only about 0.13%–0.16% parameters to the host policies and was used for all 26 tests; the frozen model also transferred to an XHand1 whose magnetic sensor model never appeared in pretraining data, raising π0.5&\#x27;s average from 48.00% to 74.17%. Technical report, weights, and code are available on Hugging Face, GitHub, and ModelScope; all results are vendor-reported without independent reproduction.

rss · 量子位 · Nov 10, 07:57

**「背景」** Before this release, tactile sensing for robot manipulation was fragmented across incompatible sensor types — visuotactile, magnetic, and piezoresistive — each producing raw signals tied to a specific sensor model and touchpoint layout, so tactile experience could not be reused when hardware changed. Prior work in this area includes per-sensor pretrained tactile models such as FTP-1, which required a dedicated adapter for each robot hand, and earlier Ant Lingbo &\#x27;embodied-native&\#x27; releases like LingBot-VLA, which targeted driving different robot embodiments from one brain but left post-contact perception to visual inputs alone. LingBot-Touch extends that line from &\#x27;one brain, many machines&\#x27; to &\#x27;one brain, many hands,&\#x27; positioning a shared tactile representation layer between sensors and arbitrary action policies.

**「Why it matters for builders」** Because LingBot-Touch sits as a frozen, hardware-agnostic layer between sensors and policies, robotics teams can retain their own action models \(the tests included third-party policies like π0.5 and Diffusion Policy\) and switch tactile sensors or hand hardware without recollecting tactile data or retraining the perception stack—an experiment where the XHand1 integration required zero changes to LingBot-Touch parameters suggests real portability across sensor layouts, though this rests solely on the vendor&\#x27;s benchmarks. The tiny parameter footprint \(5.3M for Tiny, ~0.13%–0.16% added to host policies\) also keeps it practical for on-robot compute, and open weights allow teams to evaluate the claims on their own hardware before adopting it.

### [Chips and Cheese Analyzes AMD&\#x27;s Zen 6: Core Tradeoffs and a New I/O Die](https://chipsandcheese.com/p/amds-zen-6-family-feeding-the-beast) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Chips and Cheese published a technical analysis of AMD&\#x27;s Zen 6 CPU family, covering microarchitectural changes that are not yet in shipping silicon. Reported core changes include four vector loads per cycle at 256 bits or less, two-ahead branch prediction, and a reduction in integer multiply units from three to two, with PDEP/PEXT throughput also dropping from three to two instructions per cycle. The analysis also points to denser chiplet configurations — 32 C-cores per CCD with increased L3 cache — plus expected I/O die and memory controller improvements. Because the piece analyzes unreleased hardware, the findings are expectations based on AMD&\#x27;s disclosures and analysis rather than measured retail results.

hackernews · pixelpoet · Nov 10, 17:02 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50443090)

**「Background」** Zen 6 is AMD&\#x27;s successor to Zen 5, shown on the company&\#x27;s roadmap in July 2024 and believed to be built on TSMC 6 nm and 2 nm processes. Reporting on AMD&\#x27;s desktop Zen 6 design \(codenamed Medusa Ridge\) has pointed to more cores per chiplet, larger L3 caches, and a new memory controller, which is the context for this microarchitecture analysis.

**「Why it matters」** For AM5 platform users, the most consequential item is the anticipated new I/O die on a smaller node with an improved memory controller. Commenters note the current AM5 Zen 4/Zen 5 memory controller is hard or impossible to run above roughly 3600 MT/s with both channels populated, so a Zen 6 upgrade could finally relieve that limit — though buyers should treat this as unconfirmed until AMD ships and benchmarks the part.

**「Community discussion」** Hacker News commenters debated the tradeoffs: bmenrigh called the current AM5 memory controller the platform&\#x27;s worst flaw and put an improved controller at the top of their Zen 6 wish list, while Aardwolf questioned whether the drop in PDEP/PEXT throughput shares circuitry with the reduced multiplier count. Others welcomed the balanced small improvements but asked how software should handle 32 cores per CCD within a single NUMA level.

### [Synaptics patent claims halt open-source DisplayLink driver work](https://lwn.net/SubscriberLink/1102549/acbc92e00cee3a3f/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Work on open-source DisplayLink drivers has been halted after Synaptics asserted patent claims against the project and demanded compensation, reportedly including a demand for US$25,000 to recoup costs of documenting alleged infringing acts. The claims were communicated with a compliance deadline and a threat of seeking monetary damages, according to the letter quoted in community discussion. The development affects users of DisplayLink-based USB docking stations who relied on community-maintained Linux drivers.

hackernews · speckx · Nov 10, 18:02 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50443824)

**「What led to this」** Developer Mike Lothian had been building Vino, a Rust-based open-source DisplayLink driver created through reverse engineering, after experiencing repeated crashes with Synaptics&\#x27; proprietary Linux software for its DisplayLink docking stations. Synaptics responded with cease-and-desist letters citing 125 patents and demanding compensation, after which the Vino driver project was taken offline, as reported by Phoronix and covered in early November 2026.

**「Fewer options for Linux dock users」** Users of DisplayLink docks on Linux may lose open-source driver support, leaving them dependent on Synaptics&\#x27; proprietary driver or forcing a move to alternatives such as Thunderbolt or USB4 docks that use DisplayPort alt mode with upstream Intel and AMD host support. Commenters also noted that dock vendors are reportedly scaling back DisplayLink products, which could shrink the ecosystem further regardless of the legal outcome.

**「Legal merits and relevance debated」** Commenters disputed Synaptics&\#x27; legal theory: xxpor argued that reverse engineering cannot itself infringe a patent, since patents publish the mechanism publicly, and that a trade-secret claim would likely fail against clean-room reverse engineering; AdmiralAsshat questioned whether the $25,000 documentation-cost demand could hold up in court, calling it a scare tactic. Others focused on the technology itself: kieranl claimed DisplayLink is declining economically due to high DDR prices and is superseded by better-supported Thunderbolt/USB4 docks, while cbdumas reported reliability problems such as audio issues with a DisplayLink dock.
