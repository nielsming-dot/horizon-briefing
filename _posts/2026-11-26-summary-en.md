### [PrismML&\#x27;s Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B fits a 27B coding model into 5.9 GB with ternary weights](https://the-decoder.com/prismml-squeezes-an-agent-based-coding-model-with-27-billion-parameters-into-5-9-gb/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

PrismML released Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B, a ternary-quantized version of Qwen3.8-27B in which every weight takes only one of three values \(−1, 0, +1\), compressing a roughly 54 GB full-precision model to 5.9 GB — the same footprint as its predecessor Bonsai 2. On vendor-reported agentic benchmarks, it improved from 60.8 to 75.6 percent on SWE-bench Verified \(versus 80.6 for the full-precision base\) and from 51.2 to 90.5 on AppWorld, though it scored slightly below Bonsai 2 on LiveCodeBench v6. The company says the model needs fewer passes and tokens on long tool-call chains and is less prone to reasoning loops, and it reports ~204 tokens per second on an RTX 4090 via multi-token prediction. Weights are Apache 2.0 licensed and run through llama.cpp on Nvidia and Intel GPUs and via MLX on Apple devices. These benchmark figures have not been independently reproduced.

rss · The Decoder · Nov 25, 11:51

**「Context」** Ternary quantization compresses neural networks by replacing conventional 16-bit floating-point weights with only three possible values \(−1, 0, or +1\), cutting memory roughly tenfold at the cost of precision that must be recovered through specialized training. PrismML first introduced this approach with Bonsai 27B in July, built on an older Qwen model, with its smallest variant running in under 4 GB on an iPhone. The new release builds on that foundation, moving to the newer Qwen3.8-27B base and targeting agentic workloads, where the earlier model reportedly struggled with looped or overly long reasoning chains.

**「Why it matters for builders」** At 5.9 GB with llama.cpp and MLX support, a 27B-parameter agentic coding model can run on a single RTX 4090, consumer Intel GPUs, and Apple hardware including iPhone and iPad, removing the need for datacenter GPUs for local agent workloads — provided the vendor-reported benchmark gains hold up under independent testing.

### [Reported 12-year-old pre-auth remote root flaw in Linux kernel NFS server](https://opensec.in/blog/nfs2root-cve-2026-99362) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A blog post reports CVE-2026-99362, a claimed pre-authentication remote root vulnerability in the Linux kernel&\#x27;s NFS server that the author says has existed for roughly 12 years. The report is unverified here: commenters note that no public proof-of-concept exploit has been posted, and one commenter says the issue appears to affect only NFS versions 4.1 and 4.2 and to have already been patched upstream months ago. Practical impact may be limited because NFS servers are rarely exposed to the public internet.

hackernews · dm · Nov 25, 20:35 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50647161)

**「Background」** The Linux kernel&\#x27;s NFS server daemon \(nfsd\) lets remote clients access files over the network, and since protocol versions 4.1 and 4.2 it has supported compound requests with a SEQUENCE operation used to manage cached replies. According to Red Hat&\#x27;s advisory, CVE-2026-99362 arises when a remote client negotiates a minimal cached reply size and sends crafted compound requests containing SEQUENCE operations, triggering an out-of-bounds memory write in the kernel; installations whose kernels lack the updated sequence buffer bounds check remain affected, and the fix has landed in newer kernel releases.

**「Practical impact」** Operators running kernels without the upstream fix, particularly on out-of-support distributions, can temporarily restrict exposure by disabling NFS 4.1 and 4.2 \(for example, writing &quot;-4.1 -4.2&quot; to /proc/fs/nfsd/versions after stopping nfs-server\), though this reverts on reboot and should be checked against client protocol usage first. Systems whose NFS service is bound to trusted internal networks face substantially lower risk than internet-exposed hosts.

**「Community reaction」** Commenter superkuh shared the NFS 4.1/4.2 disable mitigation above, while Gigachad argued the flaw is minimally impactful since NFS is not assumed safe for the public internet and noted the apparent earlier upstream patch. One commenter promoted a third-party XDP-based firewall tool \(&quot;yeet&quot; pfw\) as a fix; this self-interested promotion and the claimed rule were not independently verified. Others pointed to the absence of a public PoC and, more broadly, to Linux&\#x27;s large trusted computing base.

The shorter proof is described as well-documented, with human-readable documentation throughout and an explicit split between &quot;known math&quot; and &quot;new math.&quot; The work is available on GitHub, though the item does not supply details on how the shorter formalization has been reviewed or accepted.

hackernews · nb-t · Nov 25, 21:48 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50648050)

**「Background」** GRH refers to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, and the item concerns a Lean formalization that previously existed only as an enormous machine-generated artifact of roughly 17 million lines before this community rewrite compressed it to about 76,000. The debate around it echoes concerns Terence Tao has raised about machine-assisted mathematics: in a February 2025 talk he demonstrated formalizing proofs in Lean with AI assistance, and he has argued that an AI-produced answer is not a genuine solution unless it comes with a rigorous, human-comprehensible proof.

**「Why it matters」** The documentation and known-vs-new math separation should make it substantially easier for mathematicians to digest the proof&\#x27;s core argument, which was previously obscured by machine-scale proof volume.

**「Community reaction」** Commenters debated whether this challenges Terence Tao&\#x27;s recent point that huge machine proofs lacking insight are a problem: one asked whether LLMs that can &quot;golf&quot; and explain proofs already move past that concern, while another wished that mathematicians who genuinely understand the argument would present it as a conventional mathematical paper at a conference.

Europe&\#x27;s Hera mission has returned observations of the asteroid Dimorphos, giving scientists their first close-up look at the aftermath of NASA&\#x27;s 2022 DART kinetic impactor test. DART struck Dimorphos in September 2022 at more than 13,700 mph \(6.14 km/s\), releasing roughly 10 billion joules of energy and shortening the asteroid&\#x27;s 12-hour orbit around its larger companion Didymos by about 33 minutes — 25 times the most conservative expectation. rss · Ars Technica · Nov 25, 22:36

**「Why This Matters Now」** In September 2022, NASA&\#x27;s Double Asteroid Redirection Test \(DART\) spacecraft deliberately crashed into the asteroid Dimorphos at more than 13,700 mph, releasing roughly 10 billion joules of energy and shortening Dimorphos&\#x27;s roughly 12-hour orbit around its larger companion Didymos by about 33 minutes—about 25 times the most conservative expectation. **「Why it matters」** The data will let planetary defense researchers understand how kinetic impacts reshape small bodies, helping calibrate how a future deflection mission should be sized and aimed against a genuinely hazardous asteroid — a key unknown, since DART&\#x27;s orbit change far exceeded the most conservative predictions.
