### [Durvalumab-Enfortumab Vedotin Perioperative Regimen Extends Survival in Muscle-Invasive Bladder Cancer](https://www.medpagetoday.com/meetingcoverage/esmo/123570) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Perioperative durvalumab \(Imfinzi\) plus three cycles of neoadjuvant enfortumab vedotin significantly improved event-free survival \(EFS\) and overall survival \(OS\) compared with radical cystectomy in the phase III VOLGA trial of 695 patients with muscle-invasive bladder cancer who were cisplatin-ineligible \(76.1%\) or declined cisplatin \(23.9%\), with a median age of about 72; results were presented at the ESMO congress and published concurrently in the peer-reviewed New England Journal of Medicine. At 24 months, EFS was 63.5% versus 45.1% with surgery alone \(HR 0.51, 95% CI 0.38-0.67, P&lt;0.001\) and OS was 78.4% versus 69.4% \(HR 0.58, 95% CI 0.40-0.83, P=0.003\), with complete response rates of 45.1% versus 9.6%. Adding tremelimumab as a third drug improved EFS \(HR 0.61, 95% CI 0.47-0.80, P&lt;0.001\) but has not shown an OS benefit \(HR 0.77, 95% CI 0.54-1.07, P=0.12\) and produced more toxicity, with grade ≥3 adverse events in 79.5% of the triplet arm versus 71.1% with the doublet and 50.2% with control. The findings have not yet undergone regulatory review or formal guideline incorporation, and detailed safety management and sponsor/conflict disclosures were not provided in the report.

rss · MedPage Today · Oct 25, 19:37

**「Background」** Muscle-invasive bladder cancer is conventionally managed with radical cystectomy plus perioperative cisplatin-based chemotherapy, but many patients cannot tolerate or decline cisplatin — among those enrolled in VOLGA, 76.1% were cisplatin-ineligible and 23.9% refused it — and this group has historically had poor outcomes with few perioperative options. The perioperative combination of enfortumab vedotin with pembrolizumab already improved event-free and overall survival versus radical cystectomy in cisplatin-ineligible disease in the KEYNOTE-905 trial, results that were confirmed against standard chemotherapy in the cisplatin-eligible population in KEYNOTE B-15. VOLGA builds on this landscape by pairing enfortumab vedotin with a different checkpoint inhibitor, durvalumab, using only three neoadjuvant EV cycles and no adjuvant EV, reflecting the rationale of concentrating EV delivery before surgery while administering immunotherapy before and after.

**「Impact on care」** For cisplatin-ineligible patients with muscle-invasive bladder cancer, a group with historically poor outcomes and few perioperative options, VOLGA supports shifting practice from proceeding directly to radical cystectomy toward perioperative durvalumab plus three preoperative cycles of enfortumab vedotin, which raised 24-month event-free survival from 45.1% to 63.5% and overall survival from 69.4% to 78.4%. Because the doublet added grade 3/4 immune-mediated adverse events at 11% versus 20% and enfortumab vedotin discontinuations at 11% versus 23% in the triplet arm without an overall survival benefit, clinicians adopting this change should favor the doublet and counsel patients on its substantial overall grade ≥3 toxicity rate of 71.1%. A practical advantage is that restricting enfortumab vedotin to the neoadjuvant phase limited grade 3/4 peripheral neuropathy to under 1%, though formal guideline incorporation remains pending as the results undergo guideline review following concurrent peer-reviewed publication in NEJM.

### [Remibrutinib Cut Relapse Rates Versus Teriflunomide in Phase III Relapsing MS Trials](https://www.medpagetoday.com/meetingcoverage/ectrims/123572) ⭐️ 8.0/10

In two identical randomized phase III trials, twice-daily oral remibrutinib 100 mg significantly reduced annualized relapse rates versus once-daily teriflunomide 14 mg in adults with relapsing multiple sclerosis. REMODEL-1 \(n=1,000\) showed annualized relapse rates of 0.073 versus 0.112 \(rate ratio 0.648, 95% CI 0.453-0.927, P=0.018\) and REMODEL-2 \(n=1,007\) showed 0.080 versus 0.116 \(rate ratio 0.688, 95% CI 0.487-0.972, P=0.034\) among patients ages 18-55 with recent disease activity and EDSS scores of 0-5.5; the findings were presented at the joint ECTRIMS-ACTRIMS meeting and published simultaneously in The Lancet. Key secondary endpoints also favored remibrutinib, with 65-70% reductions in new or enlarging T2 lesions and 66% reductions in gadolinium-enhancing lesions versus teriflunomide, and pooled 6-month confirmed disability progression was significantly lower \(7.7% vs 10.5%, HR 0.70, P=0.038\), though 3-month progression was not \(11.7% vs 14.1%, HR 0.80, P=0.090\). The safety profile was consistent with remibrutinib&\#x27;s profile in other indications, with more bleeding events than teriflunomide \(12.7% vs 6.7%, described as mostly mild skin manifestations\) and no cases meeting Hy&\#x27;s Law criteria for drug-induced liver injury; a 25 mg formulation \(Rhapsido\) is already approved for chronic spontaneous urticaria and symptomatic dermographism, but MS approval is not yet in place and Novartis has only announced plans to seek regulatory approval globally, with teriflunomide serving as a moderate-efficacy comparator rather than a high-efficacy benchmark.

rss · MedPage Today · Oct 25, 20:49

**「Treatment landscape」** Relapsing multiple sclerosis is typically managed with oral disease-modifying therapies; teriflunomide \(Aubagio\), the active comparator in the REMODEL trials, is an established option of moderate efficacy, so a relapse-rate win against it is meaningful even though it is not the highest-efficacy benchmark in the field. BTK inhibitors are an emerging oral class in MS, but development has been complicated by liver safety: tolebrutinib, a brain-penetrant BTK inhibitor tested in four phase III MS trials including two in relapsing MS \(GEMINI 1 and 2\), received an FDA Complete Response Letter centered on serious, potentially fatal drug-induced liver injury risk. This class history frames why the reported absence of a liver signal with remibrutinib, including no Hy&\#x27;s Law cases in the REMODEL studies, is a key point of differentiation.

**「Practice Impact」** If Novartis&\#x27;s planned global regulatory submissions succeed, clinicians would gain a twice-daily oral option backed by head-to-head evidence that remibrutinib cut relapse rates by roughly a third versus teriflunomide, equivalent to approximately one relapse per 13 years. Within the trials, remibrutinib also showed fewer adverse-event discontinuations than teriflunomide \(3.1% vs 5.8%\) and less alopecia \(5.8% vs 13.5%\), tolerability measures relevant to patients who currently discontinue or poorly tolerate teriflunomide.

### [Four medical societies issue life-stage framework for women&\#x27;s heart care](https://www.statnews.com/2026/10/26/heart-health-women-four-life-stages-qa-sadiya-khan/?utm_campaign=rss) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Four medical societies — the American Heart Association, American Academy of Pediatrics, Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine, and American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists — jointly published a consensus framework in Circulation on October 26, 2026, organized as three articles that map cardiovascular care for girls and women across four life stages, from adolescence and the first period through pregnancy, perimenopause, and menopause. Writing group chair Sadiya Khan of Northwestern University Feinberg School of Medicine and her coauthors frame the effort as a response to clinical fragmentation in cardiovascular disease, the leading cause of death in women, arguing that female-specific biology requires connected care around transitions absent from male-derived care models: menstruation, pregnancy, perimenopause, and menopause. The framework singles out perimenopause — the transition of variable or skipped cycles and hormone changes from roughly age 45 to 51 — as a cardiovascular inflection point where emerging cholesterol and metabolic changes can be identified and acted on early. This is a consensus framework, not trial data or a formal practice-changing guideline, and the available coverage is an edited interview rather than the full recommendations, so specific clinical actions will depend on each specialty&\#x27;s adoption of the documents.

rss · STAT News · Oct 26, 09:00

**「Background」** Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death among women, and although female biology shapes heart health through periods, pregnancy, perimenopause, and menopause, clinical trials were not required to include female participants until 1993. Girls&\#x27; and women&\#x27;s care has since remained fragmented across specialties: obstetricians rely on specialty-specific clinical guidance from organizations like ACOG, while cardiologists use risk-assessment tools such as the American Heart Association&\#x27;s PREVENT calculator to guide preventive care, with no shared playbook connecting these stages over a lifetime.

**「Impact」** If clinicians adopt it, the framework&\#x27;s concrete effect is to replace siloed visits with a shared roadmap across pediatrics, obstetrics, and cardiology: women who develop hypertension or diabetes in pregnancy would be deliberately transitioned from postpartum care into primary and preventive care, and women around ages 45 to 51 would be evaluated for cholesterol and metabolic changes during perimenopause instead of having it treated as a purely reproductive transition. It also establishes common language about what is normal — cycle length and bleeding, menopausal symptoms — with heavy menstrual bleeding flagged because it can cause iron deficiency that limits exercise and can serve as an early flag for polycystic ovary syndrome, which the interview notes often takes years to diagnose in teens and young adults. Because the document is a consensus framework without binding recommendations, its effect on practice depends on whether individual specialties and health systems actually incorporate it.
