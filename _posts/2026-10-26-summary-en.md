### [Durvalumab-Enfortumab Vedotin Improves Survival in Cisplatin-Ineligible Bladder Cancer](https://www.medpagetoday.com/meetingcoverage/esmo/123570) ⭐️ 8.0/10

In the phase III VOLGA trial \(695 patients, 229 sites in 25 countries\), perioperative durvalumab plus neoadjuvant enfortumab vedotin significantly improved event-free and overall survival versus radical cystectomy alone in patients with muscle-invasive bladder cancer who were cisplatin-ineligible or declined cisplatin. Event-free survival at 24 months was 63.5% versus 45.1% with surgery alone \(HR 0.51, 95% CI 0.38-0.67, P&lt;0.001\), and overall survival at 24 months was 78.4% versus 69.4% \(HR 0.58, 95% CI 0.40-0.83, P=0.003\). Complete responses occurred in 45.1% of the doublet group versus 9.6% of controls, though grade ≥3 adverse events were more frequent with the doublet \(71.1% vs 50.2%\). The results were presented at the ESMO congress and published concurrently in the New England Journal of Medicine; adding tremelimumab improved event-free survival but not overall survival, with higher immune-mediated toxicity and more enfortumab vedotin discontinuations than the doublet.

rss · MedPage Today · Oct 25, 19:37

**「Background」** Muscle-invasive bladder cancer has traditionally been treated with radical cystectomy, but many patients are ineligible for or decline cisplatin-based chemotherapy, the standard neoadjuvant option, leaving them with historically poor outcomes. Perioperative therapy combining enfortumab vedotin with a checkpoint inhibitor has recently emerged as an alternative: the KEYNOTE-905 trial showed that enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab improved event-free and overall survival in cisplatin-ineligible patients, with results confirmed in the cisplatin-eligible population by KEYNOTE B-15. VOLGA extends this approach by testing durvalumab as the checkpoint inhibitor partner and by omitting enfortumab vedotin from the adjuvant phase, showing a positive result with only three preoperative cycles of the antibody-drug conjugate.

**「Practice Implication」** For cisplatin-ineligible patients—a group with historically poor outcomes and few options—the doublet offers a perioperative, cisplatin-free alternative, with only three cycles of enfortumab vedotin given before surgery, which investigators noted limited exposure to cumulative peripheral neuropathy \(grade 3/4 in &lt;1%\). Adoption will depend on guideline uptake, and the findings follow the earlier KEYNOTE-905 results for enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab in the same setting, giving clinicians more than one perioperative regimen to consider.

### [GRB 220706A: Gamma-ray burst engine stayed active for a record 27 days](https://www.space.com/science/astrophysics/record-breaker-astronomers-spot-powerful-cosmic-explosion-that-lasted-27-days) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Astronomers report that GRB 220706A, first detected on July 6, 2022, is an ultra-long gamma-ray burst whose central engine remained active for roughly 27 days in the burst&\#x27;s rest frame, producing extremely late X-ray flaring. That is about 20 days longer than any previously recorded case; the prior record for delayed flaring was around six days after the burst, according to study team member Benjamin Gompertz of the University of Birmingham. A supernova is associated with the event, confirming a collapsing star was involved, but the nature of the long-lived power source—possibly a rapidly spinning magnetar, jet interaction with dense ejected shells, or an unusually large progenitor such as a blue supergiant—remains unidentified. The findings are described in a pre-peer-reviewed manuscript, so the result should be treated as not yet peer-reviewed.

rss · Space.com · Oct 26, 17:00

**「Background」** Gamma-ray bursts are among the most energetic explosions known and come in two main classes: long-duration GRBs, thought to arise when a massive star collapses into a black hole and launches a relativistic jet, and short-duration GRBs, linked to mergers of neutron stars. GRB 220706A belongs to a rare subclass called ultra-long GRBs, in which the central engine stays active far longer than the typical seconds to minutes; the source notes that X-ray flaring after GRBs had previously been seen only within a few days of the burst, with a prior record of about six days. A comparable precedent is the ultra-long GRB 111209A from 2011, which was associated with the unusually bright supernova SN2011kl and attributed to a rapidly rotating magnetar, a highly magnetized neutron star, leading researchers to consider magnetars or interaction with dense ejected shells as possible explanations for GRB 220706A&\#x27;s late flaring and bright supernova.

**「Implications for surveys and models」** The 27-day flaring strains existing models of how GRB jets are powered and motivates theoretical work on energy sources from collapsing stars that can persist for a month. Because long-lived GRBs emit more strongly in X-rays and low-Earth-orbit satellites lose sight of targets every ~90 minutes, X-ray-sensitive missions such as the Einstein Probe are well suited to finding more ultra-long events, helping determine whether they belong to the normal GRB population or a distinct class.

### [JWST and ALMA reveal dusty, rapidly growing black hole at redshift 6.55](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-jwst-alma-reveal-dusty-rapidly.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Observations with JWST and ALMA have characterized the dust-reddened quasar J1440+0019 at redshift 6.5488, when the universe was less than a billion years old. The object hosts a rapidly accreting supermassive black hole with an estimated mass of about 1.1 billion solar masses. The findings appear in a paper posted to the arXiv preprint server on October 9, and have not yet been peer reviewed. The result adds to the growing population of massive, dust-obscured quasars known from the first billion years of cosmic history.

rss · Phys.org Space · Oct 26, 13:20

**「Background」** Dust-reddened quasars are active galactic nuclei whose central engines are hidden or partially obscured by thick dust, which dims and reddens their optical light, so JWST&\#x27;s infrared sensitivity combined with ALMA&\#x27;s millimeter observations is well suited to measuring their black hole masses and host-galaxy gas content. Objects at redshift z ~ 6.5, like J1440+0019, are seen less than a billion years after the Big Bang, and the growing census of billion-solar-mass black holes at such early times already strains standard models of supermassive black hole growth, motivating searches for obscured examples that earlier optical surveys would have missed.

**「Why it matters」** Each new measurement of a billion-solar-mass black hole at such high redshift tightens the constraints on models of how supermassive black holes grew so quickly after the Big Bang, testing scenarios involving heavy seed black holes, sustained super-Eddington accretion, or heavily obscured growth phases like the one implied by J1440+0019&\#x27;s dust reddening.
