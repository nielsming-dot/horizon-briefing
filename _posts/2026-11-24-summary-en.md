### [Ladybird Browser Reaches Alpha on macOS and Linux](https://ladybird.org/posts/alpha/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Ladybird, an independent web browser built on a from-scratch engine, launched its first alpha for macOS and Linux on Monday, announced by developer Andreas Kling after more than four years of development. It is the first browser in years to ship with an entirely new engine, entering an ecosystem where virtually all popular browsers rely on just three engines: Gecko, Blink, and WebKit. A Windows version is planned for the later stages of the project. Early testers report that many real-world websites render correctly, though this is alpha software and the project itself flags security caveats.

hackernews · janvdberg · Nov 23, 16:13 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50614249)

**「Background」** For roughly a decade, the browser market has consolidated around just three engines: Mozilla&\#x27;s Gecko, Apple&\#x27;s WebKit \(forked from KDE&\#x27;s KHTML in 2003\), and Google&\#x27;s Blink, which forked WebKit around 2013. Nearly all popular browsers today are built on one of these, so Ladybird&\#x27;s from-scratch engine is the first genuinely new browser engine to reach a public testing release in years. The project grew out of Andreas Kling&\#x27;s SerenityOS hobby operating system and spent more than four years in development before this alpha on macOS and Linux.

**「Why it matters」** For users and developers concerned about the concentration of the web on three engines—all with ties to large vendors—Ladybird offers a genuinely independent engine to test sites against. Alpha testers should treat it as experimental, not a daily driver or security-hardened browser; one commenter asked what precautions users should take given the project&\#x27;s own upfront security warnings.

**「From the discussion」** Testers reported surprisingly strong compatibility: several said tricky sites, from ad-heavy news pages to Proxmox&\#x27;s NoVNC remote-display interface, rendered correctly, with one noting only slightly jerky scrolling. Other commenters raised open questions—hoping Ladybird takes browser fingerprinting seriously with spoofed or randomized values, and asking what precautions are warranted given acknowledged security concerns—while one observed that adopting production-grade libraries like SQLite and Skia was key to the engine&\#x27;s progress.

**「Impact」** Developers who rely on the site as a fast, browsable Arm ISA reference will lose access after the December 2026 shutdown, and mirrors may face the same legal pressure. rss · Hechinger Report · Nov 24, 06:00

**「Context」** Public universities are funded partly by state tax dollars with an explicit mission of providing affordable education to state residents, and students have long been advised to stay in-state to keep costs down. But state funding levels vary widely: Florida has kept tuition low through sustained legislative funding and tuition caps over the past decade, while states like Alabama and Pennsylvania leave low-income students facing much higher net prices. The federal Integrated Postsecondary Data System \(IPEDS\) tracks average net price — full cost of attendance minus grants and scholarships — by income bracket, which is the data underpinning this analysis.

**「Why it matters」** Because flagships have some of the highest graduation and job placement rates among public universities, their cost is a decisive factor in whether low-income students can access the most reliable path to economic mobility. rss · Hechinger Report · Nov 23, 11:00
