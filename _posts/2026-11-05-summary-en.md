### [Go team unveils experimental encoding/json/v2 API](https://go.dev/blog/jsonv2) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Go team announced an experimental new JSON API, encoding/json/v2, on the official Go blog. The package redesigns how Go programs marshal and unmarshal JSON, and includes a lower-level jsontext package for token-level processing. It is experimental, so APIs may still change, and existing code continues to use the current encoding/json package.

hackernews · olexsmir · Nov 4, 17:52 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50369106)

**「Background」** Go&\#x27;s original encoding/json package has been part of the standard library since the language&\#x27;s early days and is one of its most widely used packages, but its long-standing API design has accumulated limitations that a from-scratch successor can address only by breaking compatibility. The new encoding/json/v2 is therefore being introduced as an experimental package so the Go team can gather feedback and let projects migrate incrementally before any wider adoption.

**「Impact」** Go developers can start evaluating the new API experimentally, but migration is not frictionless: commenters noted that convenience helpers like MarshalIndent are absent in v2, making equivalent code complicated and non-obvious to port. Teams needing custom error handling or high-performance parsing can already benefit from the jsontext package, which one commenter reported using in production for strict, fast parsing of malformed client input.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters broadly welcomed v2 but flagged migration pain, with CamouflagedKiwi wishing MarshalIndent had been kept because its replacement is &quot;super complicated and non-obvious.&quot; Others shared positive production experience with jsontext \(txomon\) and, more skeptically, brunoborges quipped that JSON &quot;clearly isn&\#x27;t&quot; simple given how long languages have struggled to implement it well.

rss · Ars Technica · Nov 4, 22:26

**「What it means」** Because the exploited flaws were already patched, users who keep iOS and Android devices updated were largely protected from this campaign. Users of targeted or war-related websites should ensure their devices run current OS versions, since the imposter site used a reverse proxy to serve real content mixed with malicious JavaScript, making it hard to spot visually.

The precise methodology behind the discovery is not evident from the supplied material, so it remains unclear how the vulnerabilities were found or how many have been fixed. The disclosure drew active discussion on Hacker News, where a team member confirmed authorship and answered questions.

hackernews · dangelosaurus · Nov 4, 23:33 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50372929)

**「Background」** Local privilege escalation flaws let an attacker who already has ordinary user access on a Linux machine elevate to root, and they have long been assumed to exist in the kernel in significant numbers. OpenAI&\#x27;s disclosure states that an autonomous research pipeline found, validated, and generated patches for 948 such vulnerabilities in a single run, after reproducing 1,724 candidate flaws.

**「Impact」** Organizations running multi-user Linux systems should treat local root escalation as a near-default assumption rather than an edge case: with roughly 948 disclosed escalation paths in the kernel, any user-level compromise can plausibly be upgraded to full root access, making host-level controls such as immutable or minimal OS images, container isolation, and prompt patching more consequential. The disclosure also signals that AI-assisted vulnerability discovery is now producing large-scale kernel findings, as seen in recent AI-assisted Linux kernel privilege escalation disclosures throughout 2026, so defenders should expect the pace of such reports to increase rather than decline. Operators should prioritize applying kernel security updates promptly and review whether systems that assume kernel integrity between users still hold.

**「Community reaction」** Commenters questioned how the kernel accumulated so many escalation paths, asking whether the problem stems from complexity, the security model, or language-level flaws, with one noting that local escalation in Linux was long assumed possible but that the figure of 948 is still shocking. Another commenter asked what mitigating factors keep this level of vulnerability from being an existential operational threat. A team member \(dangelosaurus\) confirmed the work is their team&\#x27;s and invited questions, noting the team is hiring.

### [IEEE Spectrum: AI vulnerability discovery is exposing decades-old software flaws](https://spectrum.ieee.org/ai-in-cybersecurity-php2shell-vulnerability) ⭐️ 7.0/10

An IEEE Spectrum report examines how AI-driven vulnerability discovery is uncovering security flaws that have accumulated in widely used software over decades, including PHP-to-shell exploit classes. The piece frames the situation as a payoff of roughly 40 years of technical debt, but leaves open a key question the community is actively debating: whether AI auditing leads to a one-time remediation of old flaws or a continuous maintenance treadmill of feature-neutral patching. The article is analytical rather than announcing a specific new tool or disclosed vulnerability, and no independently verified count of newly discovered flaws is given.

hackernews · pseudolus · Nov 4, 11:52 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50364336)

**「Background」** The framing of AI forcing payment of decades-old technical debt follows the recent &quot;PHP2Shell&quot; incident: on October 21, 2026, the PHP Foundation issued emergency patches for two critical PHP core vulnerabilities \(CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619\) allowing unauthenticated remote code execution, in PHP—a language created in 1994 and still widely running legacy sites. The flaws&\#x27; exploitability was demonstrated in practice on October 25, when researcher Jaydip Modhwadia achieved remote code execution on an unpatched PHP server during the &quot;Steal My Bitcoin&quot; online security challenge. The episode illustrates how AI-assisted discovery is surfacing vulnerabilities in long-lived software that had gone unexamined for years.

**「What this means for maintainers and users」** Organizations running open source components should expect more previously unknown vulnerabilities to surface as AI auditing becomes routine, increasing patch workload in the near term. A commenter also argues the assumption that open source code is secure because &\#x27;someone else audited it&\#x27; was often false, and that AI auditing could finally make broad independent review practical—suggesting teams review their dependency audit practices rather than relying on that assumption.

**「Debate: debt payoff or endless treadmill」** Commenters split on the long-term outcome: one argues many flaws are small, narrowly patchable bugs suggesting a finite debt payoff, while another contends many issues require restructuring, making the fix workload potentially open-ended. Others expressed hope that AI discovery will incidentally surface stockpiled nation-state zero-days, and one claimed that the &\#x27;someone else has audited this code&\#x27; assumption about open source security was long overstated and is now being corrected.

### [Ry Herman on Queer Fairy Tales and ATU 514](https://lithub.com/ry-herman-on-finding-inspiration-in-queer-fairytales/) ⭐️ 4.0/10

Literary Hub published an essay by Ry Herman, excerpted from the author&\#x27;s book &quot;This Knight Topples Empires&quot; \(Dial Press, 2026\), on researching LGBTQ+ themes in fairy tales. Herman describes finding a folkloric tradition with explicitly queer content, catalogued in the Aarne-Thompson-Uther Index as tale type 514, &quot;The Shift of Sex,&quot; in which a heroine disguised as a man marries a woman and is finally transformed into a man. Examples include the ancient Greek myth &quot;Iphis and Ianthe,&quot; the Nart saga &quot;Alimbeg&\#x27;s Daughter,&quot; the Serbian tale &quot;A Woman Became a Man,&quot; and the Finnish story &quot;Satu.&quot; Herman argues that many of these tales are less queer-positive than they appear, since retellings in hostile periods often turned the transformation into an imposed curse that restored traditional gender roles, and explains that this research shaped the nonbinary protagonist Jules in their own book.
